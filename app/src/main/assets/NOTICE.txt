Word frequency lists
Arabic contains 25,000 selected entries from FrequencyWords' 2018 50k list. English starts with its 25,000 selected entries, refreshes frequencies from the OpenSubtitles2018 training slices below, adds 288 whole contractions, and includes lowercase SCOWL English/American/British spellings (125,674 asset entries, plus the spelling-only supplements below). SCOWL words absent from the training slice receive the minimum frequency so they are preserved, not promoted as frequent completions. Non-word tokens are filtered; files are tab-separated.

SCOWL spelling vocabulary
Source: SCOWL 2020.12.07, https://wordlist.aspell.net/ and https://downloads.sourceforge.net/wordlist/scowl-2020.12.07.tar.gz.
English, American and British word lists through size 70 plus upper-case and abbreviation lists through size 60 validate additional English spellings; proper-name and variant lists are not imported. Modifications: lowercase and union these lists, retain only ASCII letters of length 2..32, and assign frequencies from the separately licensed OpenSubtitles training slice (minimum 1 when unobserved).

Arabic spelling protection
known_forms_ar.txt contains 429,720 valid surface forms generated from the CAMeL Morph MSA LREC-COLING 2024 database, revision 15f5aede4b609db54abd6f87aded1f5ec5d930af. It is separate from the frequency lists and only prevents destructive correction; it does not rank new completions or supply typo targets. Modifications: generate compatible inflections without extra clitics, omit proper-name/backoff parts of speech, strip harakat and tatweel, map alef wasla to alef, retain Arabic-letter forms of length 2..32, sort and deduplicate.

Small curated spelling supplements
The runtime additionally recognizes respawn (an independently curated English gaming term), آراء, مرفقات, إخطار and إفراغ (attested in the CAMeL data credited above), the productive definite forms المرفقات and التجميع (base تجميع), and corpus-attested تعاملك with its productive ل-prefixed form لتعاملك. These use the minimum lexical score 1, not measured corpus counts. They enable manual completion/typo choices; unobserved Arabic supplements are not evidence for automatic orthographic replacement. CAMeL-derived supplements retain CC BY 4.0 attribution and terms above. Small keep-as-typed overlays protect deliberate technical/brand/loanword spellings, including npm, Romex, كويز and سيفتي; they do not claim observed frequencies or endorsement by trademark owners. باكيت and الباكيت are curated low-tier dialect spellings, not measured frequency estimates.

Next-word predictions
next_en.tsv and next_ar.tsv contain observed totals and follower counts for 30,000 one-word contexts; context_en.tsv and context_ar.tsv contain 60,000 two-word contexts each. Rows retain up to eight followers. A <s> row stores sentence-start counts. The desktop replay datasets are excluded verbatim from training.
English training uses compressed byte ranges [0,125000000) and [150000000,325000000) of OpenSubtitles2018; Arabic uses [0,125000000) and [145000000,400000000). The intervening ranges supply held-out benchmark text. These tables and the refreshed English frequencies are distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0.
jordanian.tsv and jordanian_next.tsv combine the original hand-curated vocabulary/continuations, cleaned Shami corpus Jordanian text, and independently generated synthetic Jordanian chat messages. Shami observations are weighted three times relative to synthetic observations; jordanian_next.tsv continuation counts use only the training split of Shami (about 79% of lines, grouped in 50-line blocks) while the rest is held out for benchmarking, and jordanian.tsv vocabulary still comes from the full corpus; the synthetic text is not a measured human corpus. Original curated rows receive explicit synthetic rank-based counts. The dialect setting enables these extra predictions and completions.
Shami: Kathrein Abu Kwaik, Motaz Saad, Stergios Chatzikyriakidis, and Simon Dobnik (2018), "Shami: A Corpus of Levantine Arabic Dialects", LREC 2018. Source: https://github.com/GU-CLASP/shami-corpus (Data/jordinian.txt). Its repository is licensed under Apache License 2.0; the bundled adaptations strip mentions, URLs, emoji and Arabic marks and extract word frequencies and n-gram counts. License text: assets/licenses/shami-Apache-2.0.txt.
Rebuild scripts, source attribution and sampling limitations: bench/lm/SOURCES.md. Benchmark-only Tatoeba and spelling-error datasets are not packaged or used to generate the keyboard assets.
Source: OPUS, https://opus.nlpl.eu/OpenSubtitles/corpus/version/OpenSubtitles (P. Lison and J. Tiedemann, 2016).
